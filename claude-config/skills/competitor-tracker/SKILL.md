# Competitor Tracker

## Overview

This skill structures and executes competitive analysis frameworks. It tracks competitor positioning, messaging, pricing, and features, analyzes their advertising strategies and creative approaches, builds comparison matrices and SWOT analyses, identifies market gaps and opportunities, and produces actionable competitive intelligence reports.

## When to Use This Skill

- When entering a new market and need to understand the competitive landscape
- When preparing sales battlecards or competitive positioning documents
- When analyzing why deals are being lost to specific competitors
- When planning product roadmap priorities based on competitive gaps
- When reviewing competitor ad strategies before launching a campaign
- When presenting competitive intelligence to stakeholders or investors

## How It Works

1. Identify the competitive set (direct, indirect, and aspirational competitors)
2. Gather intelligence across defined dimensions
3. Organize findings into structured frameworks (comparison matrix, SWOT)
4. Analyze patterns, gaps, and opportunities
5. Produce actionable recommendations

## Instructions

### Step 1: Define the Competitive Set

Categorize competitors into three tiers:

- **Direct competitors**: Same product/service, same target market
- **Indirect competitors**: Different product but solving the same problem
- **Aspirational competitors**: Market leaders you aim to compete with long-term

For each competitor, capture:
- Company name and website
- Founded year and company size (employees, funding)
- Primary market and target audience
- One-line positioning statement

Limit analysis to 4-6 competitors for depth over breadth.

### Step 2: Analyze Positioning and Messaging

For each competitor, document:

**Brand Positioning**:
- Tagline and headline messaging
- Primary value proposition
- Key differentiators they claim
- Target audience they address in their copy
- Tone and voice (professional, casual, technical, aspirational)

**Messaging Hierarchy**:
- Primary message (homepage hero section)
- Secondary messages (feature pages, about page)
- Proof points (case studies, testimonials, data claims)
- Call-to-action language and urgency tactics

Present as a comparison:

```
| Element        | Competitor A        | Competitor B        | Our Brand          |
|----------------|---------------------|---------------------|--------------------|
| Tagline        | "Scale faster"      | "Simple and smart"  | "Growth made easy" |
| Primary value  | Speed               | Ease of use         | Simplicity + power |
| Target tone    | Enterprise/formal   | SMB/casual          | Mid-market/friendly|
| Key proof      | "10K+ enterprises"  | "Set up in 5 min"   | TBD                |
```

### Step 3: Compare Features and Pricing

Build a feature comparison matrix:

```
| Feature            | Us  | Comp A | Comp B | Comp C |
|--------------------|-----|--------|--------|--------|
| Core Feature 1     | Yes | Yes    | Yes    | No     |
| Core Feature 2     | Yes | No     | Yes    | Yes    |
| Advanced Feature 1 | Yes | Yes    | No     | No     |
| Integration X      | No  | Yes    | Yes    | Yes    |
| API Access         | Yes | Paid   | Yes    | No     |
```

Use these markers: Yes, No, Partial, Paid add-on, Beta, Coming soon.

**Pricing Analysis**:
- Pricing model (per seat, per usage, flat rate, freemium)
- Entry price point and enterprise pricing
- Free tier limitations
- Contract requirements (monthly vs. annual)
- Discounting patterns observed

### Step 4: Analyze Ad Strategies

Review competitor advertising across channels:

**Ad Presence**:
- Which platforms they advertise on (Google, Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube)
- Estimated monthly ad spend (use tools like Meta Ad Library, SEMrush, or SimilarWeb)
- Ad frequency and seasonality patterns

**Creative Analysis**:
- Ad formats used (static image, video, carousel, lead gen)
- Visual style and branding consistency
- Headline and copy patterns
- Offers and promotions (free trial, discount, demo)
- Landing page structure and conversion approach

**Keyword Strategy** (for search ads):
- Branded keyword bidding (do they bid on our brand?)
- Top non-branded keywords
- Ad copy messaging themes
- Landing page relevance and quality

### Step 5: Build SWOT Analysis

For each key competitor, create a SWOT:

```
COMPETITOR A: SWOT Analysis
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Strengths:
  - Strong brand recognition in enterprise market
  - Extensive integration ecosystem (200+ integrations)
  - Well-funded ($50M Series C)

Weaknesses:
  - Complex onboarding, steep learning curve
  - Pricing excludes SMB market
  - Slow feature release cadence

Opportunities:
  - They are ignoring the mid-market segment
  - No mobile app available
  - Customer complaints about support response times

Threats:
  - Their new AI features could close our gap
  - Strategic partnership with major platform announced
  - Aggressive hiring in our key markets
```

### Step 6: Identify Gaps and Opportunities

Synthesize the analysis into actionable findings:

**Market Gaps**:
- Underserved segments no competitor addresses well
- Features frequently requested but not available
- Pricing tiers that leave customers without good options
- Geographic markets with limited competition

**Positioning Opportunities**:
- Claims competitors make that we can counter with proof
- Weaknesses we can exploit in our messaging
- Categories or narratives we can own

**Threat Assessment**:
- Competitors most likely to take our customers and why
- Emerging competitors to watch
- Market trends that could shift competitive dynamics

### Step 7: Produce the Intelligence Report

Structure the final report as:

1. **Executive Summary** (3-5 bullet points with the most important findings)
2. **Competitive Landscape Overview** (market map showing positions)
3. **Detailed Competitor Profiles** (one page per competitor)
4. **Comparison Matrices** (features, pricing, positioning)
5. **SWOT Analyses** (per competitor)
6. **Opportunities and Recommendations** (prioritized action items)
7. **Monitoring Plan** (what to track ongoing, review cadence)

## Best Practices

- Update competitive intelligence monthly for fast-moving markets, quarterly for stable ones
- Distinguish between facts (observable data) and assumptions (inferences)
- Source information from public data: websites, ad libraries, job postings, press releases, review sites, SEC filings
- Track competitor job postings to infer strategic priorities (hiring AI engineers = investing in AI)
- Monitor review sites (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot) for unfiltered customer sentiment
- Focus analysis on dimensions that affect buying decisions, not vanity metrics
- Present findings with "so what" implications, not just raw data
- Create a shared competitive intelligence repository that the whole team can access
- Avoid confirmation bias -- document competitor strengths honestly
- Include a "what we can learn from them" section for each competitor
- Set up Google Alerts and social listening for real-time competitive updates
